## Stage 0: Legend fix

**What changed** (`js/core.js`, `createPairDice` and `rollForBoard`):
- **The mix:** Legend deals a teen × 2–9 for half of its rolls and Tricky facts for the rest.
- **The size bias:** all pair dice (Tricky, Master, Legend) make big rectangles rarer on smaller boards, like the loaded dice on Easy, Medium and Hard do.
- **The fallback:** near the end, when none of the difficulty's facts fit the gaps left, the pair dice deal a smaller fact (up to 12 × 12) that does fit, so the board gets finished instead of both players passing.
- **Board size:** Legend now needs a 12×12 board or bigger (invites too).

Measured, rectangles per CPU-vs-CPU game (a unit test guards the minimums):

| Board | Hard | Tricky before → after | Legend before → after | Teen facts per Legend game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12×12 | 11.7 | 4.1 → 10.0 | 2.7 → 8.7 | 1.8 |
| 16×16 | 12.8 | 6.5 → 13.6 | 3.9 → 12.7 | 2.7 |
| 20×20 | 15.1 | 9.9 → 18.8 | 5.5 → 16.9 | 3.8 |
| 24×24 | 19.1 | 13.3 → 23.2 | 7.7 → 21.5 | 5.1 |

**Decisions made on your behalf:**
- **Teen share is 1/2, not the 1/3 in the plan.** With 1/3, the size bias and the end-of-game fill-in left only about 1 teen fact in a 12×12 game; 1/2 gives about 2 without making games shorter. It's `LEGEND_TEEN_SHARE` in core.js.
- **Tricky had the same problem** (4 rectangles on 12×12), so the size bias and fill-in apply to every pair-dice difficulty, not just Legend.
- **The fill-in facts can be easier than the difficulty** (for example 2 × 3 on Tricky). The alternative was ending games with a third of the board empty.

**How to try it:** single player → Legend (or type IDDQD after pressing ~ to unlock everything) → 12×12 or bigger → play. The hint under the board size explains the mix.
